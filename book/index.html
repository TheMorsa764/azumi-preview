Yoshuku 予祝

The Japanese Art of Manifesting

Azumi Uchitani

A practical, beautifully illustrated guide to the ancient Japanese art of manifesting and pre-celebration.

Purchase the book Learn about Yoshuku

Read about the book

予

In a world of constant motion, where desires and dreams often feel just out of reach, “Yoshuku” offers a path to manifestation rooted in ancient Japanese wisdom.

It introduces you to the gentle power of collective intention and pre-celebration — a practice that not only shapes your future but uplifts those around you.

Azumi Uchitani, an internationally acclaimed cultural communicator, unveils the secrets of Yoshuku—a long-established Japanese approach to bringing your deepest wishes into reality. With the same quiet profundity found in concepts like wabi-sabi, Yoshuku teaches us that the key to manifesting lies not in relentless pursuit, but in mindful anticipation and shared joy.

予祝
What is Yoshuku

Pre-celebration

予 — in advance祝 — celebration

Yoshuku celebrates gratitude for what we are yet to have. Azumi reveals how Japanese wisdom, life philosophy, and traditional customs of yoshuku, or “pre-celebration,” can uplift and enrich not only the lives of those who practise it but everyone around them, too.

Key ideas

Four ideas at the heart of the book

01

Pre-celebration

Yoshuku celebrates gratitude for what we are yet to have — celebrating a wish as though it has already come true.

02

Collective intention

A practice that not only shapes your future but uplifts those around you. Manifesting is something we do together.

03

Mindful anticipation

The key to manifesting lies not in relentless pursuit, but in slowing down and tuning into the rhythms of the universe.

04

Shared joy

Learn how to integrate ancient rituals with a modern lifestyle, immerse yourself in harmony, and uncover the secrets of acceptance in order to manifest your soul’s wishes.

From the book

After the typhoon

During a particularly tough season of life, a Buddhist monk told Azumi that the most beautiful sakura cherry blossoms emerge in the spring following the fiercest typhoons; it’s only because of the storms that the trees endure, invigorate, and develop deeper roots to survive. This is true for humans, too: while we are on our journeys to blossom, we will face hardships, but those are the very opportunities for growth that we need to lean into.

About the author

Azumi Uchitani

Japanese author, artist, cultural communicator and educator, raised with the teachings of Shingon Buddhism and Shinto, the tea ceremony and calligraphy. A TEDx speaker twice over, she is based in Bussum, the Netherlands, and works internationally. The US edition carries her own cover art.
